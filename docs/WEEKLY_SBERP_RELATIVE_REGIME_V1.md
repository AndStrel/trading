# Frozen protocol: weekly SBERP relative-strength and market regime v1

This is a local historical experiment, not an order instruction. Freeze this protocol before computing outcomes. Only SBERP is traded; SBER and IMOEX supply lagged signals. The hypothesis is that SBERP outperforming ordinary SBER over the prior 63 sessions, within a rising SBERP annual trend and a positive broad-market regime, raises the fraction of profitable weeks after costs.

Use the existing verified MOEX ISS daily TQBR SBER, SBERP and IMOEX 2015–2025 files. On the first observed weekday session each week, use only preceding closes. Enter SBERP at that session's open if (1) its previous close exceeds the close 253 observed sessions earlier, (2) its close-to-close return from 64 sessions earlier exceeds SBER's over the same interval, and (3) the prior IMOEX close exceeds the average of the last 200 IMOEX observed closes. If history is stale by more than seven calendar days, do not enter. Exit at the open 20 SBERP observed weekday sessions later. At most five simultaneous positions, each sized at no more than one fifth of marked equity. Follow the existing `simulateWeeklyMarketRegime` implementation unchanged, supplying SBERP as its traded series and SBER as its comparator; the market index remains IMOEX. No optimization of thresholds or holding period after results.

Base execution costs: 0.3% commission and 0.05% slippage per side. Stress slippage: 0.1% per side. No leverage, dividends, tax, market impact, or live fills are modeled. Use both completed-trade outcomes and `auditWeeklyPortfolio` marked positive active weeks. Compare with the already defined SBERP always-long 20-session control. Do not touch 2026 data.

Minimum research gate: at least 100 completed trades across 50 entry weeks; at least 51% net winning completed trades **and** 51% positive active portfolio weeks; positive mean net return, profit factor above 1, positive sum after removing the best five trade returns, and positive annual mean for every traded year. Inspect each of 2016–2019, 2020–2022, 2023–2025 separately and reject missing years. Even if these exploratory checks pass, independent historical validation and a fuller execution/corporate-action audit are required before any trading discussion.
