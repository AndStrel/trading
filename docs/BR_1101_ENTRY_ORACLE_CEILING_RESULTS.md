Of 133 ordinary dates, 131 had the fixed 11:01 entry and every later holding minute through 16:55; two were missing and not scored. Assuming impossible knowledge of the best future minute high/low, the **long** side had 108/131 (82.4%) base-net-positive days, 102/131 (77.9%) under stressed execution, with +0.9774% mean base oracle return. The **short** side had 111/131 (84.7%) base-net-positive days, 106/131 (80.9%) under stress, with +1.3998% mean base oracle return. These are separate fixed-direction *upper bounds*; the report does not choose the better direction on each date.

This shows that BR's post-11:01 **range**, unlike the previously studied SBER/GAZP noon-entry control, is often large enough to cross the assumed 0.10%-commission plus adverse-execution round trip. It does **not** show that a trader can anticipate direction, fill at a later extreme, control interim losses, or earn these returns. The separately tested [first-hour continuation](BR_FIRST_HOUR_V1_RESULTS.md) won only 55/131 (42.0%) base-net trades and had negative mean. Therefore the next useful question is predictive information and feasible risk-controlled exits, not a post-hoc search for the best exit minute on these same 2026 paths. Full RUB P&L, live bid/ask, margin and actual fills remain unverified. No order was placed.
